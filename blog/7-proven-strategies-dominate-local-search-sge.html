7 Proven Strategies to Dominate Local Search in the Google SGE Era

📅 August 29, 2026 ⏱ 18 min read 🏷️ SGE • AI Search • Local SEO • GBP

The local search landscape has fundamentally shifted. These 7 strategies are backed by 1.3M+ citations and 5.47M real search queries.

The local search landscape has undergone its most profound transformation since Google introduced the Local Pack over a decade ago. Between 2024 and 2026, Google's transition from a search engine to an AI-powered answer engine has fundamentally rewritten the rules of local business visibility.

This paper synthesizes findings from over 1.3 million citations, 5.47 million search queries, 60,000 real-world simulations across 4,423 businesses in 20 countries, and 322 local markets tracked over 18 months. The evidence is unambiguous: traditional local SEO alone no longer guarantees visibility. Businesses that adapt to AI-driven discovery are winning market share; those that do not are becoming invisible.

40%+ of local search queries now trigger AI Overviews
58% drop in organic CTR when AI Overviews appear
69% of all searches now end without a single click

Introduction: The Great Disruption

For over two decades, local search followed a predictable pattern. A user searched for a service "near me," Google displayed a map with three business listings (the Local Pack), and users clicked through to websites or called directly. That model is rapidly becoming obsolete.

Enter AI Overviews — formerly known as Search Generative Experience (SGE). These AI-generated summaries sit atop search results, synthesizing information from multiple sources and answering questions directly on the page. As BrightLocal CEO Brad Wetherall observed after speaking at BrightonSEO San Diego, "Google is no longer just curating information; it's synthesizing it. And in doing so, it's reshaping how consumers discover and engage with local businesses."

The scale of this shift is staggering. Google AI Overviews now appear in over 40% of local search queries, with some studies showing penetration as high as 68% of local-intent searches. A Sterling Sky study tracking 322 local markets found that 88% of markets lost business visibility when AI-powered local packs replaced standard results. Organic click-through rates for top-ranking pages dropped by 58% when AI summaries appear. And a staggering 69% of all searches now end without a single click.

This is not a temporary fluctuation. This is the new baseline.

The purpose of this paper is to provide local business owners, marketers, and SEO professionals with seven evidence-based strategies to not just survive but dominate in the Google SGE era. Each strategy is grounded in empirical research from authoritative sources including Ahrefs, Semrush, Seer Interactive, Local Falcon, Sterling Sky, BrightLocal, Whitespark, and Google's own Search Central documentation.

The New Reality: How AI Searches for Local Businesses

Before examining the seven strategies, it is essential to understand how AI systems actually process local search queries. This understanding informs every subsequent strategy.

When a user searches "best Italian restaurant near me" or "emergency dentist in Austin," Google's AI does not pull from a single source. It synthesizes information from multiple data streams:

  1. Google Business Profile data — hours, address, phone number, reviews, categories, attributes, and photos become raw material for the AI-generated overview
  2. Structured data (Schema markup) — LocalBusiness, Event, and Offer schema create machine-readable signals that AI systems prioritize
  3. Authoritative web content — pages that rank well for location-specific keywords get cited heavily in AI Overviews
  4. Review signals — review velocity, recency, content depth, and responses influence both local pack visibility and AI Overview inclusion
  5. Proximity — businesses closer to the searcher appear in AI Overview results 72.0% of the time, compared to 68.5% for those slightly farther away
🔍 Critical Insight: Only about 17% of sources cited in AI Overviews also rank in the organic top 10. This means businesses that dominate traditional search results are not automatically cited in AI-generated answers. Conversely, businesses that may not rank #1 organically can still appear in AI Overviews if their content is structured for AI consumption.

Furthermore, Google's AI Mode and AI Overviews reach the same conclusions 86% of the time for a given search query—but cite almost entirely different sources, with only 13.7% citation overlap. This fragmentation means businesses need to optimize for multiple AI surfaces simultaneously.

DAC's study of over 700 real SERPs across four major verticals revealed another critical insight: only 1% of queries triggered both AI Overviews and the traditional Maps Pack in the same SERP. When both appeared, the AI Overview came first, pushing the Maps Pack below the fold. AI Overviews dominate informational queries (queries beginning with "how," "why," "what" triggered AIOs 28% of the time), while Maps Packs dominate transactional or locational queries.

With this foundation established, we now turn to the seven strategies.

Strategy 1: Transform Your Google Business Profile into an AI-First Asset

The Strategic Imperative

Your Google Business Profile is no longer just a listing—it is now the single most critical piece of your online presence. As BrightLocal's research makes clear, "Your Google Business Profile... is what powers the Knowledge Panel, and the Knowledge Panel is fast becoming the place where decisions are made."

When users click on a business name or image in AI Mode, they are taken not to the business website but directly to the GBP interface within search results. Even photos and videos are rendered directly in the panel. This means your GBP has effectively become your new homepage—a homepage you do not fully control but must meticulously optimize.

The Evidence

Businesses that optimized their GBP for AI visibility appeared in local AI Overviews 40%+ of the time, compared to those that did not. The Local Falcon study of 60,000 simulations across 4,423 businesses found that proximity—which is fundamentally a GBP signal—determines whether a business appears in AI Overview results at all.

Implementation Framework

Complete Every Section of Your Profile

BrightLocal's research emphasizes: "Fill out every attribute, even if the answer is 'No.' For instance, if a business isn't woman-owned, still mark that attribute as 'No' rather than leaving it blank." Incomplete profiles are ignored by AI systems.

Optimize Descriptions and Services with Intent

Use clear, keyword-rich language that mirrors the way people ask questions. AI systems prioritize content that directly answers user queries. Instead of writing "We offer plumbing services," write "Emergency plumber available 24/7 for burst pipes, clogged drains, and water heater repair in [city]."

Select Precise Categories and Attributes

Choose the primary category that most closely matches your core service and add secondary categories for each additional service. Use attributes (e.g., "family-friendly," "outdoor seating," "women-led") to provide nuanced information that AI systems can match to specific user intents.

Use Real Photos and Videos—and Update Them Often

AI systems prioritize fresh, authentic visual content. Businesses that upload new photos weekly signal active management, which influences AI citation decisions.

Post Regularly to Influence Conversions

Regular GBP posts do not directly influence rankings but create engagement signals that AI systems interpret as business activity and relevance.

Maintain NAP Consistency

Ensure your name, address, and phone number are identical across your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, and all industry-specific directories. Inconsistencies confuse AI systems and reduce citation likelihood.

Strategy 2: Implement Comprehensive Structured Data (Schema Markup)

The Strategic Imperative

In 2026, businesses without comprehensive schema markup are invisible to AI search. Structured data is no longer a "nice to have" for local SEO—it is a prerequisite for AI citation.

Google's official guide to optimizing for generative AI features explicitly recommends reviewing structured data, noting that "Schema markup for local businesses, services, FAQs, and reviews helps AI systems parse and extract information accurately."

The Evidence

Research on LLM positional bias reveals that content appearing in the first third of a page receives disproportionate citation attention. One analysis of 1.2 million ChatGPT responses found that 44.2% of citations originated from the first 30% of source text—consistent with Stanford's "Lost in the Middle" findings on how language models allocate attention.

Structured data ensures that your most important information appears early and in a machine-readable format, increasing the likelihood of AI citation.

Implementation Framework

Implement LocalBusiness Schema

At minimum, every local business needs LocalBusiness schema markup that includes:

  • Business name, address, phone number
  • Geo-coordinates (latitude/longitude)
  • Opening hours
  • Service area
  • Accepted payment methods

Implement Service Schema

Each service or product offering should have its own Service schema, including:

  • Service name and description
  • Price range
  • Service area
  • Provider details

Implement FAQPage Schema

FAQ schema is particularly valuable because AI systems prioritize question-answer formats. BiggerBite's research confirms that "businesses showing up in AI Overviews share common traits: comprehensive FAQ content, strong LocalBusiness schema markup, high review counts with recent activity, and content that directly answers common questions."

Implement Review Schema

Review schema helps AI systems understand the volume, recency, and sentiment of your reviews—all factors that influence AI citation decisions.

Implement Geo-Specific Schema

BiggerBite recommends "geo-specific schema that helps AI understand your service areas down to the neighborhood level." This is particularly valuable for businesses serving multiple neighborhoods within a city.

📄 The Emerging Standard: llms.txt — A new standard is emerging: llms.txt files that help AI systems understand your business. Similar to robots.txt for search crawlers, llms.txt provides structured information about services, locations, and expertise that AI assistants can reference. Early adopters are seeing increased citations in ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity responses.

Strategy 3: Build Authority Through Review Velocity and Response

The Strategic Imperative

Reviews have always mattered for local SEO, but in the AI era, they have become a primary signal for AI citation decisions. Google's AI now reads the text of reviews and replies—not just the star average—and pulls recurring themes into the AI Overviews that sit above the local pack.

The Evidence

BrightLocal's 2025 data shows that businesses responding to reviews within 24 hours receive an average ranking boost of 3 positions. Review characteristics like click-throughs, content depth, and freshness contribute to both local pack visibility and AI Overview inclusion.

According to Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors report, review signals account for 20% of local ranking weight—making them the second most important category after GBP signals (32%).

BrightLocal's 2025 Consumer Review Survey found that 76% of people watch video content when researching local businesses and 83% of consumers use Google to find local reviews.

Implementation Framework

Build a Process for Consistent Review Volume

Consistency matters more than occasional spikes. Develop a systematic approach to requesting reviews from every customer. The businesses winning in AI search are those with "consistent, recent reviews."

Respond to All Reviews—Positive and Negative

Responding to reviews within 24 hours signals active management to AI systems. Even negative reviews, when handled professionally, demonstrate trustworthiness and engagement.

Encourage Detailed, Substantive Reviews

AI systems prioritize review content depth. Reviews that mention specific services, staff members, or experiences provide richer data for AI synthesis. Reviews that are clicked, read, and expanded generate engagement signals that may impact local rankings.

Monitor Review Themes

AI Overviews pull recurring themes from reviews. If multiple reviews mention "fast service" or "friendly staff," those themes are more likely to appear in AI-generated summaries. Actively monitor and encourage reviews that reinforce your key differentiators.

Leverage Video Reviews

With 76% of consumers watching video content when researching local businesses, video reviews and responses provide a competitive advantage in AI-driven discovery.

Strategy 4: Shift from Keyword Density to Conversational, Question-Based Content

The Strategic Imperative

Traditional SEO focused on keyword density and backlinks. AI-driven search prioritizes clear, question-based answers that mimic conversational queries. This represents a fundamental shift in how content should be structured.

The Evidence

DAC's study found that queries beginning with question words ("how," "why," "what") triggered AI Overviews 28% of the time, while Maps Packs appeared in less than 1% of such queries. This means informational, question-based queries are now primarily answered by AI, not by traditional local packs.

Google's official guidance emphasizes "the importance of providing valuable, unique, non-commodity content" and "tips about providing local, shopping, image, and video content." The guidance explicitly addresses local content, acknowledging that "generative AI responses can include product listings, product information, and information about local businesses."

The shift from SEO to Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) marks a fundamental change. As research into GEO strategies suggests, "visibility is now determined by 'citation frequency' and 'source credibility' rather than just keyword density."

Implementation Framework

Create Distinct Local Service Pages

Create separate, distinct pages for each service and each location. As GEO research recommends, "creating local pages distinctes et les associer à un store locator clair pour aider les IA à comprendre l'implantation du réseau."

Structure Pages to Answer Questions Directly

Instead of writing "We offer plumbing services in Chicago," write "What is the average cost of emergency plumbing in Chicago?" followed by a direct, data-backed answer. AI systems prioritize content that directly answers user questions.

Only Include One Service Per Page

LocaliQ's research emphasizes: "Only include one service per page." Multi-service pages confuse AI systems trying to determine what you actually offer.

Create Good Pricing Pages

"Create good pricing pages" with "usable pricing information" that AI systems can extract and cite. Transparency about pricing increases citation likelihood.

Add Staff Bios to Boost Visibility Signals

"Add staff bios to boost visibility signals." Staff credentials, experience, and expertise demonstrate E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)—factors that Google explicitly cites as important for AI source selection.

Write Content AI Can Quote

Generative Engine Optimization requires "writing content AI can quote." This means clear, declarative sentences that provide definitive answers, not ambiguous or qualifying language.

Strategy 5: Navigate the AI Overview vs. Maps Pack Divergence

The Strategic Imperative

AI Overviews and Maps Packs serve fundamentally different query types. Understanding this divergence is essential for allocating optimization resources effectively.

The Evidence

DAC's study of 700+ real SERPs revealed a stark divergence:

  • Queries beginning with question words triggered AIOs 28% of the time, but Maps Packs less than 1%
  • "Near me" queries triggered Maps Packs 100% of the time and AIOs less frequently
  • Only 1% of queries triggered both features in the same SERP

Whitespark's 2026 research found that AI Overviews now appear on 68% of local-intent searches, while local packs appear on 39%. AI Overviews are becoming the dominant local search surface.

The local pack itself sees reduced click-through when an AI Overview appears above it. This means even businesses that rank in the Maps Pack may see reduced traffic if an AI Overview precedes it.

Implementation Framework

Optimize for Both Surfaces, but Prioritize Based on Query Intent

For informational queries ("how," "why," "what"), prioritize AI Overview optimization through FAQ content, structured data, and authoritative content. For transactional queries ("near me," "best [service] in [city]"), maintain Maps Pack optimization while also building AI citation signals.

Create Content That Triggers Both

While rare, some queries trigger both features. Create content that serves both informational and transactional intent—for example, a page that answers "How much does [service] cost in [city]?" while also providing clear calls to action.

Monitor AI Overview Trigger Rates for Your Industry

Restaurant queries jumped from 10% to 78% AI Overview trigger rates in a short period. Some industries are more exposed to AI disruption than others. Service businesses like personal injury attorneys and creative professionals exhibit "higher volatility, making AI Overview visibility harder to predict or control."

Don't Abandon Maps Pack Optimization

AI Overviews cite sources from the top ten organic results in 99.5% of cases. Strong Maps Pack rankings still correlate with AI citation likelihood, even if the correlation is imperfect.

Strategy 6: Become a "Cited Source" Through Multi-Platform Presence

The Strategic Imperative

Visibility in AI search is now determined by "citation frequency" and "source credibility." Businesses that appear across multiple AI platforms—Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot—build cumulative citation authority that reinforces visibility across all platforms.

The Evidence

An AI Search Visibility Report analyzing how 7 leading local-SEO brands are cited found that Google AI Overviews and AI Mode together account for roughly 700 citations—more than ChatGPT and Copilot combined.

Brand-owned websites earn 31% of AI Overview citations. Pages cited in an AI Overview see approximately 18% fewer organic clicks on average, but cited domains see 12% more direct traffic and 9% more brand search. This means AI citations build brand awareness even when they don't generate immediate clicks.

Businesses cited inside AI Overviews earn 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks compared to those that are not cited.

Implementation Framework

Build Citations Across All Major AI Platforms

Generative Engine Optimization requires being visible wherever decisions begin. This means optimizing for Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini simultaneously.

Maintain Consistent Signals Across Platforms

"Brands that appear early in AI-indexed data and maintain consistent signals get cited more often as training data stabilizes." First-mover advantage is real—businesses that establish presence early benefit from citation momentum.

Publish Everywhere AI Looks

Uberall's GEO Studio recommends: "Publish Everywhere AI Looks." This means maintaining presence across Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, industry directories, social media, and your own website—all with consistent, accurate information.

Leverage PR and Media Coverage

Even a little bit of PR can go a long way, especially in smaller geographies where the competitive field is thinner. Media mentions create authoritative third-party citations that AI systems trust.

Consider Dual-Language Content for Non-English Markets

"Dual-language content is a must for non-English markets. It helps AI systems connect the same business to multiple ways of asking the same local question."

Strategy 7: Optimize for "Zero-Click" Discovery and Direct Conversion

The Strategic Imperative

With 69% of all searches ending without a click and AI Overviews reducing organic CTR by 58%, the old model of "rank → get click → convert" is broken. Businesses must optimize for zero-click discovery—being found and chosen without the user ever visiting the website.

The Evidence

According to a 2024 BrightLocal consumer survey, 58% of local searchers now interact with the AI Overview before scrolling to organic results, and 23% never scroll at all. The decisions are being made within the AI-generated summary, not on your website.

BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that consumer use of AI for finding local businesses jumped from 6% to 45% in March 2026 alone. This represents a more than sevenfold increase in a single year.

Ahrefs' February 2026 analysis found that AI Overviews reduce organic clicks by 58%—nearly double the 34.5% reduction measured just eight months earlier. The freefall is accelerating, though it may be stabilizing into a new baseline.

Implementation Framework

Ensure All Critical Information Is in Your GBP

Since users are increasingly interacting with the GBP interface rather than your website, every piece of information a customer might need must be in your Google Business Profile: hours, address, phone, services, pricing, photos, and FAQs.

Optimize for Direct Actions

The local pack is a zero-click format that leads directly to calls, navigation, and website visits—"hoher Geschäftswert trotz fehlendem organischen Klick" (high business value despite the lack of an organic click). Ensure your phone number is click-to-call enabled, your address is click-to-navigate enabled, and your booking links work flawlessly.

Monitor and Optimize AI Overview Impressions

Google's official guidance recommends opening Google Search Console and "checking which pages have AI Overview impressions." Understanding where you are already visible in AI results helps prioritize optimization efforts.

Audit Your Most Important Service Pages

Google's official guide recommends auditing "your five most important service or product pages" for AI optimization. Ensure these pages directly answer the questions customers actually ask, not just keyword phrases.

Avoid AI-Generated Content

Google's guidance warns against "filling the site with 'AI-made' or 'AI-optimized' content. Prioritize authentic and complete content." AI systems are trained to detect and penalize low-quality AI-generated content. Authenticity remains paramount.

Conclusion: The New Rules of Local Search

The evidence assembled in this paper points to five inescapable conclusions:

First, the transition from search engine to answer engine is not theoretical—it is happening now. AI Overviews appear in over 40% of local searches, and penetration is accelerating. The old "ten blue links" model is being systematically replaced by AI-generated summaries that answer questions directly on the search results page.

Second, visibility in AI search is not determined by the same factors as traditional SEO. Only about 17% of sources cited in AI Overviews also rank in the organic top 10. Businesses that dominate traditional search results cannot assume they will dominate AI results.

Third, the Google Business Profile has become the most critical asset for local businesses. It is no longer just a listing—it is the primary source document for AI generation and the interface through which most local search decisions are made.

Fourth, review velocity, recency, and content depth have become primary signals for AI citation. Businesses that actively manage their review profile and respond promptly to feedback gain a measurable competitive advantage.

Fifth, the shift from SEO to GEO represents a fundamental change in how businesses must think about visibility. Success now depends on becoming a "cited source" that AI systems trust and reference, not just ranking for keywords.

The businesses that will dominate local search in the coming years are not those with the largest SEO budgets or the most backlinks. They are the businesses that understand how AI systems think, structure their data accordingly, and build the trust signals that AI systems prioritize.

The rules have changed. The question is not whether you will adapt—it is whether you will adapt quickly enough.
